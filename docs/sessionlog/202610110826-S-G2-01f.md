## S-G2-01f · 2026-10-11 · assembly + policy — who convenes holds the office; the agenda, the date and the posting act are the sign-in's (PM-GA-002, PM-GA-007, PM-SEC-001)

**Shipped** — the second of three slices answering the audit (findings 2 and 3). Convening an assembly takes its convenor from the sign-in, and the office stated — `MB`, `BM`, `CTL` — must be one that party holds in the entrance today: a mandate in force, asked of `policy` (a new action `CONVENE_ASSEMBLY`, three lines, matrix version 6). The request loses `convenedBy`. The agenda and the date of an office-convened assembly are run by whoever holds an office today; an assembly the owners convened on their petition is run by the owner who convened it, while they still own. The posting act is recorded by the convenor signed in and by nobody for them, and its co-signatory must be an owner in the entrance on the day of posting, checked through `registry.Holdings`. The assembly row and the posting act name the sign-in that made them. A new guarded layer, `AssemblyActs`, asks the policy before anything is looked up.

**Verified** — `ActingTest` (5) over the real policy and dated roles · `AssemblyWebTest` (7) with tokens signed by the test's own key, the real `WhoAsks`, `Policy`, `Acting` and `AssemblyActs` over mocked services · `AssemblySignInOffWebTest` (1) · `NoticeServiceTest` proves the co-signatory and the convenor's own act · `PolicyTest` pins the matrix at version 6. On this machine's Postgres 16 from scratch copies, none skipped: `AssemblyPersistenceIT` (7) with real sign-ins, mandates and titles — the audit's scenarios each refused: a convenor named in the body by a login tied to no party, a stranger, an owner, the cashier and a former manager; an agenda item or a move on a NOTICED assembly by anyone but an office holder; a former owner co-signing — and `ProxyPersistenceIT` (2), `PetitionPersistenceIT` (3). Ten mutants killed: always allow · an office-convened assembly run by anybody the policy allows something · an owners' assembly run by another owner · the office stated not checked against the roles held · the posting act recorded by somebody else · the co-signer not checked · the co-signer checked on another day · an assembly of another entrance run through this one's path · the convening login misrecorded · a cashier's line in the matrix.

**Decisions** — the owner's standing answer, applied: **with sign-in switched off nothing is convened, put on an agenda, moved or posted**; an assembly is still read. Mine, stated on #191: the office is the one stated and held today — a person holding two convenes in either · an office-convened assembly is run by any current office holder, so a successor carries on a predecessor's draft; an owners' assembly by its convenor only · the posting act's recorder is the convenor in person — a successor in office cannot record it for them (they convene anew, or the convenor signs in) · the co-signatory stays a field of the request: the act is the convenor's record of a paper that person signed · "one owner" is checked as an owner on the day of posting, with `TODO(legal): PM-GA-007`.

**Existing rows** — no deployment holds real data; nothing was migrated. The new columns are nullable with `NOT VALID` checks, so a hand-written old row cannot fail the migration and every new row must name its sign-in.

**Open** — who added each agenda item, and who moved a meeting, is not recorded: only the convenor and the posting act's recorder are (an audit trail under PM-SEC-004 is a slice of its own) · reading an assembly is open to anyone the api lets in · a refusal is not entered in any log · a body that cannot be read is a 400 before anybody is asked who they are · slice g: proxies still take every party from the body.
