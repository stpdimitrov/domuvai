## S-G2-01f · 2026-10-11 · assembly + policy — who convenes holds the office; the agenda, the date and the posting act are the sign-in's (PM-GA-002, PM-GA-007, PM-SEC-001)

**Shipped** — the second of three slices answering the audit (findings 2 and 3). Convening an assembly takes its convenor from the sign-in, and the office stated — `MB`, `BM`, `CTL` — must be one that party holds in the entrance today: a mandate in force, asked of `policy` (a new action `CONVENE_ASSEMBLY`, three lines, matrix version 6). The request loses `convenedBy`. An assembly is then run — its agenda, its date, its posting act — by the one who convened it, in person, while they still hold what they convened in: the same office, or ownership for an assembly the owners convened on their petition. Nobody else touches it. The posting act is the convenor's own signature, and its co-signatory must be an owner in the entrance on the day of posting, checked through `registry.Holdings`. The assembly row and the posting act name the sign-in that made them. A new guarded layer, `AssemblyActs`, asks the policy before anything is looked up.

**Verified** — `ActingTest` (5) over the real policy and dated roles · `AssemblyWebTest` (7) with tokens signed by the test's own key, the real `WhoAsks`, `Policy`, `Acting` and `AssemblyActs` over mocked services · `AssemblySignInOffWebTest` (1) · `NoticeServiceTest` proves the co-signatory and the convenor's own act · `PolicyTest` pins the matrix at version 6. On this machine's Postgres 16 from scratch copies, none skipped: `AssemblyPersistenceIT` (7) with real sign-ins, mandates and titles — the audit's scenarios each refused: a convenor named in the body by a login tied to no party, a stranger, an owner, the cashier and a former manager; an agenda item or a move on a NOTICED assembly by anyone but an office holder; a former owner co-signing — and `ProxyPersistenceIT` (2), `PetitionPersistenceIT` (3). Fifteen mutants killed: always allow · an assembly run by somebody who did not convene it · by its convenor after they left the office · by an owner-convenor who sold (one mutant here survived the first run — a seller who had become the manager was not tested — and a case was added that kills it) · an office that convenes nothing stated by somebody who holds it · an old row with no sign-in carried on · an owners' assembly run by another owner · the office stated not checked against the roles held · the posting act recorded by somebody else · the co-signer not checked · the co-signer checked on another day · an assembly of another entrance run through this one's path · the convening login misrecorded · a cashier's line in the matrix.

**Review** — fresh context, security-minded, six findings. Taken: **any office could void another office's notice** — I had let any current office holder run an office-convened assembly, so the manager could add an agenda item to what the control board convened, again and again, and it would never become `NOTICED`; and once a predecessor's notice was voided nobody could post again. An assembly is now run only by its convenor in person, while they hold the office they convened in · a capacity the code does not know runs nothing (it fell through to the offices) · which roles are offices that convene is read from the matrix in the guard, not from a second list · an assembly convened before a convening named its sign-in is refused with a 409 instead of failing its write on the new check · tests for the convenor who left office, the owner-convenor who sold, and an owner who is also the manager. Filed for `identity-org`: #193 (two overlapping mandates of one office are both in force).

**Asked of the owner, through the coordinator** — once convened in an office, who may change an assembly and record its posting act: the convenor's office (convenor or successor — recommended), the convenor in person only (built here, the strictest: it only refuses more), or any office (what the review broke). **Decisions** — the owner's standing answer, applied: **with sign-in switched off nothing is convened, put on an agenda, moved or posted**; an assembly is still read. Mine, stated on #191: the office is the one stated and held today — a person holding two convenes in either · pending the owner's answer, a successor in office does not carry on a predecessor's assembly: they convene anew · the co-signatory stays a field of the request: the act is the convenor's record of a paper that person signed · "one owner" is checked as an owner on the day of posting, with `TODO(legal): PM-GA-007`.
