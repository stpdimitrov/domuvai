## S-G2-01e · 2026-10-11 · assembly + policy — who signs a petition is the sign-in's party, and the policy decides (PM-GA-003, PM-SEC-001)

**Shipped** — the first of three slices answering the audit's finding 2 ("`assembly` makes no access decision; every actor comes from the request body"). Opening a petition, signing it and convening on it now take their actor from the sign-in — the party the api's own record ties the token's login to (`identity_org.WhoAsks`) — and the request bodies no longer carry `openedBy`, `partyId` or `convenedBy`; a body that still names one changes nothing. What that party may do is asked of `policy`, as at today in Sofia: two new actions, `SIGN_PETITION` and `CONVENE_ON_PETITION`, each allowed to an owner of the entrance on the day and to nobody else. Matrix version 5. A refusal is a 403 naming the rule; the refused caller reaches nothing behind it. A person signs for themselves only. Each signature and each unlock also records the sign-in that made it — issuer and subject — beside the party it was tied to that day.

**The cut** — e (this): petitions · f: convening as `MB`/`BM`/`CTL` with the office in force, the agenda, moving the meeting, the posting act and its co-signatory an owner that day (findings 2, 3) · g: proxies (finding 4).

**Verified** — `ActingTest` (3) over the real policy and dated roles; `PetitionWebTest` (5) with tokens signed by a key of the test's own, so the application's decoder and the real `WhoAsks` and `Policy` run; `PetitionSignInOffWebTest` (1); `PolicyTest` pins the matrix at version 5 and proves the two lines; `PetitionPersistenceIT` (3; on this machine's Postgres 16 from a scratch copy, none skipped) repeats what the audit could do — a login tied to no party naming owners in the body, the manager, an owner next door, a former owner, no token — and finds one signature stored: the opener's own. Eleven mutants killed: always allow · a party with no sign-in acting · a signature recorded under another login · a login with no party acting · the UTC day for the Sofia day · the opener, the signatory and the convenor not taken from the guard (three) · the matrix line given to a holder of use · an office added to it.

**Review** — fresh context, security-minded, five findings; no way to open, sign or convene as another party on the three paths was found. Taken: **a signature named the party and not the login** — an administrator could tie one login to owner after owner and sign as each, leaving signatures indistinguishable from the owners' own; signatures and unlocks now name the sign-in, and the tables refuse one that does not · a party with no sign-in behind it is refused too (no caller can make one today) · nobody signed in as a party is refused under PM-SEC-001, not under the petition's rule · a test that an owner of another entrance, through their own entrance's path, finds the petition missing on sign and convene · the two matrix lines carry `TODO(legal): PM-GA-003`: that a holder of use may not demand an assembly is pending counsel, not settled law · the controller says a body that cannot be read is a 400 for anyone.

**Decisions** — the owner's standing answer for the registry, applied: **with sign-in switched off nobody is asking, so nothing is opened, signed or convened** — signatures and unlock records are insert-only; a petition is still read in that mode. Mine, stated on #186: only `OWN` carries the two lines — not a holder of use (the unanswered counsel question stays unanswered), not an office as such, not the administrator · the policy is the gate and the service keeps its own checks (an owner on the day through `Holdings`, once, a signatory who still owns) · `PolicyTest` names `Policy.MATRIX_VERSION` where it had the literal 4; the one pinning test still states the number.

**The audit's finding 7, answered and not acted on** — a `petition_unlock` is a stored legal determination: it says a threshold was met on a day, and an assembly stands on it. It carries the catalogue and engine versions and the dated constant, but no `basis` and no `basis_hash` of the holdings that were weighed, so a later correction of the book leaves nothing to show what was counted. Under ADR-001's amendment it is of the same kind as a stored decision, and I think it should carry both — a slice of its own, with a new migration; nothing was changed here.

**Existing rows** — no deployment holds real data, so no forged signature exists; nothing was migrated.
