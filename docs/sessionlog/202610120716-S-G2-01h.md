## S-G2-01h · 2026-10-12 · assembly — the office that convened runs the assembly; its convenor may record a written proxy (PM-GA-002, PM-GA-007, PM-GA-009)

**Shipped** — the owner's two answers of 2026-10-11 to what slices f and g left open; the strictest reading had been built meanwhile, and this lets in what the owner allowed. **An office-convened assembly is run by that office**: by the convenor while they hold the office they convened in, and once they no longer do, by whoever holds that same office in the entrance today — the agenda, the date and place, and the posting act, which that person signs as its convenor. Another office never touches it; an owners' assembly stays with its convening owner. **A proxy** is appointed by the principal signed in, for themselves, or recorded by whoever runs the assembly for the principal a written authorisation names — with the document's SHA-256 and who recorded it on the row. Every check on who the principal and the agent are applies to both ways. Recording a proxy for somebody else is a line of the policy's matrix (`RECORD_PROXY`, version 8). Who holds an office on a day is asked of the policy, and its answer comes from `identity_org`'s mandates and their one reading of "successor" — nothing about succession is worked out in `assembly`.

**Asked of the owner, through the coordinator** — three, each built at its strictest meanwhile: (1) **"successor"**: `identity_org` publishes who holds a role on a day, not who succeeded whom, and its lane is held (#202). This slice reads a successor as whoever holds the convenor's same office today once the convenor no longer does. That differs from `Mandate.succeeds` in four cases — board co-members step in once the convening member leaves; a later controller carries on though `succeeds` knows no controller's successor; a manager following a board (or the reverse) runs nothing here though `succeeds` calls them the successor; a successor whose predecessor's term is not stamped ended is refused until it is. Either `identity_org` publishes the successor question and `assembly` asks it, or the owner confirms "any current holder of the same office" · (2) may the recorder of a written proxy be the proxy it names (built: no) · (3) must the signer of a posting act have held the office on the posting day (built: yes).

**Decisions** — the owner's, 2026-10-11, through the coordinator: both above. Mine, stated on #203: "successor" is whoever holds the convenor's office today once the convenor no longer does — a co-member of a board does not step in while the convening member still sits · the posting act's `convenor_party_id` is who signed it; the assembly's `convened_by` never changes · the written authorisation is on the record as its hash, like the posting act's photograph; keeping the document is `evidence`'s · a proxy appointed in person carries no document and one recorded for somebody always does — the table holds both.

**Open** — for counsel, unchanged: whether one board member is the board for convening, and whose household a household member is · a recorded proxy is the recorder's statement that a document exists: nothing reads the document · still unguarded: reads; refusals are not logged · the petition and convening requests still ignore a body naming another actor instead of refusing it.
